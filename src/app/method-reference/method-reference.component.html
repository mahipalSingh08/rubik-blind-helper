📖 3-Style Methods Reference

Understanding commutators for both Corners (UFR) and Edges (UF)

3-Style Overview

What is 3-Style? Instead of solving one piece at a time using long setups and standard permutations (like Old Pochmann or M2), 3-Style solves two pieces at once using short sequences called commutators.

A commutator cycles 3 pieces: the Buffer, Target 1, and Target 2. By solving two targets at once, you drastically reduce your move count and eliminate long setup moves.

How Commutators Work

Commutators follow a very specific structure written as [A, B].

  • [A, B] expands to: A B A' B'
  • A is an insertion move (usually 3 moves, e.g., R U R')
  • B is an interchange move (usually 1 move on a parallel slice, e.g., D')

For example, the corner commutator [R U R', D'] becomes: R U R' D' R U' R' D.

Setup Moves with Commutators

Sometimes you cannot build a pure [A, B] commutator directly. In these cases, you use a setup move (or moves), denoted as S [A, B] S'.

You apply the setup S, perform the commutator [A, B], and then undo the setup with S'.

For example: [U: [R D R', U2]] expands to: U (R D R' U2 R D' R' U2) U'.
